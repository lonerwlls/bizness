**Title:**
The Man With a Window in His Stomach 🪟

**Description:**
1822, Mackinac Island. A musket went off by accident inside a fur-trading store and shot young Alexis St. Martin in the stomach. Army surgeon Dr. William Beaumont didn't think he'd survive the night.
He lived. But the wound never fully closed: it left an opening straight into his stomach.
Back then, nobody really knew how digestion worked. So Beaumont tied food to silk strings, lowered it through the opening and pulled it out hour after hour to see what was left. He even showed that stomach juice dissolves meat in a jar, all by itself: the stomach digests with acid, not just by grinding.
He did more than 200 experiments. St. Martin got so fed up that he ran away to Canada for years. Beaumont got him back.
St. Martin outlived his doctor by 27 years. When he died in 1880, his family buried him extra deep so no doctor could take his stomach.
