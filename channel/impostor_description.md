## Длинный ролик (8:03) — `videos/impostor_full.mp4`

**Title:**
He Faked Being a Surgeon… and Operated on 16 Men

**Description:**
Korea, 1951. On a Canadian warship, the surgeon pulls a bullet out of a soldier's chest. Sixteen wounded men, sixteen operations, and nobody dies. There's just one problem: he isn't a surgeon. He never went to medical school. A few months earlier, he was a monk.

This is the true story of Ferdinand "Fred" Demara, the Great Impostor. He was a monk, a psychologist, a college dean, a law student, a teacher and a navy surgeon. Then a prison officer, a counselor, and finally a hospital chaplain. Almost every one of those jobs ran on someone else's name.

Real facts in this video:
• Fred ran away from home at 16 to become a monk. He went on to try the army, the navy and three more monasteries.
• As "Dr. Robert Linton French" he taught psychology in Pennsylvania and became dean of a school of philosophy.
• As Brother John Payne he helped start a college in Maine. It still exists today as a university in Ohio.
• He borrowed the papers of a real young doctor, Joseph Cyr, and joined the Royal Canadian Navy in 1951 as Surgeon-Lieutenant Joseph Cyr.
• On HMCS Cayuga he pulled the captain's tooth, operated on wounded soldiers, and read the textbook first.
• The real Dr. Cyr read about "his" adventures in the newspaper. That's how Fred was caught.
• The navy didn't charge him. It released him and paid him several hundred dollars in back pay.
• He sold his story to Life magazine (January 1952). Years later an inmate in a Texas prison, where Fred worked as an officer under another name, found an old copy of that magazine.
• Robert Crichton's book "The Great Impostor" became a bestseller, and in 1961 Tony Curtis played Fred in the movie.
• In his last years Fred was a hospital chaplain in California. In 1980 he sat with Steve McQueen. He died in 1982 at sixty.

Some sailors later said the operations didn't happen quite the way the newspapers told it. But the legend stuck.
