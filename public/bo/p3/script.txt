Day four. The supermarket doors don't open, because they're electric. So you squeeze through a gap and step inside. It's dark, and it smells awful. The freezer aisle has turned into a swamp of melted ice cream and rotting fish. But the good stuff is still on the shelves. Canned beans. Rice. Peanut butter. Crackers. Bottled water. Canned food can last for years, so this is treasure. You fill a shopping cart and push it home through empty streets, and it's the loudest sound in the whole city. Day five, you build a base. A low floor, close to the exit, with big windows for daylight. You drag in mattresses, blankets and every flashlight you can find. Batteries become money. Day six, it rains. You put buckets on the roof and collect water for free. Day seven. One week alone. You start talking out loud just to hear a voice. You name the pigeons on your balcony. And you realize the hardest part of surviving isn't food or water. It's the silence.
