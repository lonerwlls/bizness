It's beautiful. It's also the loneliest thing you've ever seen. Day four. You need food that won't rot, so you walk to the supermarket. Its doors don't open, because they're electric. So you squeeze through a gap and step inside. It's dark, and it smells awful. The freezer aisle has turned into a swamp of melted ice cream and rotting fish. You cover your nose with your shirt and keep going. Because the good stuff is still on the shelves. Canned beans. Rice. Peanut butter. Crackers. Bottled water. Canned food can last for years, so this is treasure. You fill a shopping cart to the top and push it home through empty streets, and its squeaky wheels are the loudest sound in the whole city. Day five, you build a base. You pick an apartment on a low floor, close to the exit, with big windows for daylight. You drag in mattresses, blankets, and every flashlight you can find. You stack the cans along the wall like bricks. Batteries become money. And on the wall, next to your bed, you draw five little lines in chalk. One for every day.
