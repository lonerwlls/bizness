Day two. You wake up to the same silence, and for a second you think it was all a dream. Then you turn on the tap. Only a thin trickle comes out, and then it stops. City water is pushed through the pipes by electric pumps, and without power, the pressure slowly dies. So you grab every pot, bottle and bucket you can find, and you fill the bathtub with the last of it. That water is now the most valuable thing you own. Then you open the fridge. It's warm. Food in a closed fridge stays safe for only about four hours without power. A full freezer lasts around two days. So tonight is a feast. Ice cream, steak, frozen pizza cooked on a camping stove from the closet. Everything that's about to go bad. By day three, the smell in the kitchen tells you the party is over. And then the first real night arrives. With no street lights, the city is darker than you've ever seen it. You can't see your own hand. But when you look up, the sky is full of stars, thousands of them, hidden your whole life behind the glow of the city.
