Day two. You turn on the tap, and only a thin trickle comes out. Then it stops. City water is pushed through the pipes by electric pumps, and without power, the pressure slowly dies. So you fill every pot, bottle and bathtub you can find. Then you open the fridge. It's warm. Food in a closed fridge stays safe for only about four hours without power. A full freezer lasts around two days. So tonight is a feast. Ice cream, steak, everything that's about to go bad. By day three, the smell in the kitchen tells you the party is over. And then the first real night arrives. With no street lights, the city is darker than you've ever seen it. You can't see your own hand. But when you look up, the sky is full of stars, thousands of them, hidden your whole life behind the glow of the city. It's beautiful. It's also the loneliest thing you've ever seen. Tomorrow, you need food that won't rot.
