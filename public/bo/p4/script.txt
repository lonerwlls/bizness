Day six. It rains. And for the first time, rain feels like a gift. You put buckets, pots and a kiddie pool on the roof, and by evening you have clean water for free. You boil it anyway, just to be safe. Day seven. One week alone. The city is changing in small ways. Trash piles up. Shop windows are covered in dust. Somewhere, a car alarm screams until its battery dies, and then it's quiet again. You start talking out loud, just to hear a voice. You narrate what you're doing. You argue with yourself about dinner. You give names to the pigeons on your balcony. The fat one is Gary. Scientists have found that being alone for a long time can mess with your mood, your sleep, and even your memory. Humans are built to be around other humans. So you make rules to stay sane. Wake up at the same time. Exercise every morning. Keep a diary. Go outside every day, even if there's nobody to see. You realize the hardest part of surviving isn't food or water. It's the silence.
