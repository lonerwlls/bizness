Day eight. You decide you need power, and the city is full of it, if you know where to look. Every parked car has a battery. You pop the hood of a car on your street, and with a cheap inverter from the hardware store, that one battery can charge your phone again and again. You turn your phone on with shaking hands. Your photos. Your messages. But no signal. The cell towers had backup batteries, but those died days ago. Day nine, you raid a garden store and grab every solar lamp you can carry. You line them up on the windowsill in the sun. By night, your base glows like a tiny village. For the first time in a week, you're not sitting in the dark. Day ten, you build something crazy. A bicycle on a stand, connected to a small generator. Pedal for an hour, and you get enough power to run a radio. Your legs burn, but it works. You scan every station, slowly, one by one. Static. Static. Static. Nobody is talking. Or maybe nobody can hear you.
