Day nineteen. Things go wrong. A storm rolls in, and lightning hits a building three blocks away. With no fire trucks and no water pressure, nobody can stop it. You watch the fire jump from roof to roof all night, glowing orange against the black city. By morning, a whole block is gone. Day twenty, you cut your hand on broken glass while searching a store. It's deep, and it won't stop bleeding. In a world with no doctors, a small infection can become dangerous. So you wrap it in your shirt and head to the hospital. Its backup generators ran out of fuel long ago. The hallways are pitch black, and every step echoes. Your flashlight shows empty beds, open doors, and charts still hanging on the walls. In the pharmacy, you find bandages and antiseptic. You clean the cut, wrap it tight, and hope. Day twenty-two, you get a fever. For two days, you lie on your mattress, drinking rainwater, too weak to stand. You talk to the faces on the wall. Some of them start to look like your friends.
