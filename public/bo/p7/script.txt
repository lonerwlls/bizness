Day twenty-five. You climb to the roof of the tallest building you can reach. Thirty floors of stairs, with no elevator. From the top, you can see the whole city. Not a single light. Day twenty-six, you paint a giant SOS on the roof, each letter as big as a car. Pilots are trained to look for signals like that. Day twenty-seven, you collect mirrors from every bathroom in the building. On a sunny day, a mirror flash can be seen from many kilometers away. Day twenty-eight, you carry up car batteries, cables and the brightest lamps you can find, and you build a beacon. At night, it's the only light for kilometers in every direction. Day twenty-nine, you turn on the bicycle radio one more time. Static. Static. And then, for one second, a voice. Broken. Far away. But real. You pedal like crazy, shouting into the microphone that you're here, that you're alive, that someone is still in the city. The radio goes quiet. You don't sleep that night.
