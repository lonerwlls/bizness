Day fifteen. A deer walks down the middle of the highway like it owns the place. Nature comes back faster than we think. After just two weeks, weeds push through the cracks in the sidewalk. Birds sing so loud in the morning that they wake you up. The air smells cleaner than it ever has. Without cars and factories, the sky turns a deeper blue. Day sixteen, you decide to have some fun. You walk into an empty stadium and shoot hoops alone, with fifty thousand empty seats watching you. You score, and nobody cheers. Day seventeen, you sit in the best seat of a movie theater, in total darkness, eating stale popcorn, and you imagine the film in your head. It's a pretty good movie. Day eighteen, it hits you hard. You haven't seen another human face in eighteen days. You catch yourself staring at a poster of a smiling family at a bus stop for a whole minute. So you go home and start drawing faces on the wall of your base. Big ones. Smiling ones. So that someone is always looking back at you.
