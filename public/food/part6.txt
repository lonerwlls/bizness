Six months later, the world doesn't look destroyed. It just looks... smaller.
Half the windows in your building are dark. Families have moved to the countryside, or in with relatives, to share whatever food they have left.
The ones who stayed have learned to cook together. One big pot in the courtyard. Everyone brings something. Nothing gets thrown away. Not a peel. Not a bone.
The ration box got smaller. Twice. People are thinner now. Last week, you had to make a new hole in your belt.
Doctors are seeing things they had only read about in textbooks. Kids with bleeding gums. Old people too weak to stand in line.
Farms have fences now, and guards at night. A field of potatoes is worth more than a bank.
And yet, in the middle of all this, something strange happens. Neighbors who never said hello now know each other's names.
Because when there isn't enough food, there's one thing you can't survive without... each other.
