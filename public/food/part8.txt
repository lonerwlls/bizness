One year later, it's spring again.
In the courtyard, the plan on the wall has become a real garden. Rows of lettuce, beans and potatoes. Everyone knows which bed is theirs.
The first ships are back. Not hundreds. A few dozen. Trucks are moving again, slowly, with guards at the warehouses and long lines at every gas station.
One morning, the supermarket on the corner opens its doors. The shelves are half empty. A loaf of bread costs five times what it used to. But there is bread.
Nobody runs in anymore. People walk in slowly, take only what they need, and thank the cashier.
The café reopens too. Three things on the menu. Every table is full.
And you pick your first tomato. Small, a little crooked... and the best thing you've ever tasted.
The world didn't end. But nobody here will ever look at a full shelf the same way again.
