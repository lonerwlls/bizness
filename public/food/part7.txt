Nine months later, winter arrives. And winter is the hardest test of all.
Nothing grows. The balcony gardens are frozen solid. The potatoes from autumn have to last until spring, and everyone knows exactly how many are left.
There's no fuel for heating, so families move into one room. Then into one apartment. Six people around a single little stove, cooking on the same fire that keeps them warm.
You've learned to do the things your grandparents did. Drying apples on a string. Salting cabbage in jars. Keeping seeds in a paper envelope, like they're made of gold.
Then one morning, the radio says something new. The first grain ship has left port. Just one. Escorted, half empty, and weeks away.
In the courtyard, someone pins a hand-drawn plan to the wall. The spring garden. Who plants what. Who waters. Who keeps watch at night.
It's not much. But for the first time in nine months, people talk about the future... instead of the next meal.
