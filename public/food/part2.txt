Twenty-four hours later, nothing looks wrong. Not yet.
The café still opens at seven. The bakery still smells like bread. But the delivery truck that comes every single morning... doesn't come.
In ports from Rotterdam to Shanghai, hundreds of ships are waiting at anchor, full of grain, fruit and frozen meat that nobody can unload.
Highways are lined with parked trucks. Drivers sit in their cabs, waiting for orders that never arrive.
At the supermarket, you notice small gaps. No bananas. No fresh milk. A handwritten sign on the shelf says: delivery delayed, sorry for the inconvenience.
The cashier just shrugs. "They say tomorrow."
On the news, experts promise it's a technical problem that will be fixed in a day or two. Most people believe them. Why wouldn't they?
But a few people start doing something strange. They buy two bags of rice instead of one. Then three.
And the person behind them in line... notices.
