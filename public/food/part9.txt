Eighteen months later, the shelves are full again. Almost like before. But the world underneath them has changed.
Countries now keep grain reserves that can last for months, not weeks. Huge new silos stand outside every big city.
Ports keep backup systems on paper, so a computer failure can never stop the food again.
On rooftops, greenhouses grow tomatoes and lettuce for the people living right below them.
Kids learn at school how a seed becomes a meal. And your courtyard garden is still there. Nobody wanted to give it up.
In every kitchen, there's now a small shelf that never gets empty. Rice. Beans. Water. Enough for two weeks.
And on Sundays, the neighbors still eat together. The long table in the courtyard never went back into storage.
You still order pizza sometimes. You still get lazy. But every time you open a full fridge... for one second, you remember.
