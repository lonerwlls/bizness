One week later, the shortage has a new name. Crisis.
Supermarkets that were half empty are now completely empty. Some don't bother opening anymore. Others are guarded.
So governments step in. Food from warehouses and emergency reserves is collected, counted, and handed out.
You get a ration card. One card per family. One line per neighborhood. One box per week.
Inside the box: rice, beans, flour, and a few cans. Enough to survive. Not enough to ever feel full.
Restaurants close by the thousands. Delivery companies have nothing left to deliver.
Millions of people who sold, cooked, or carried food for a living... suddenly have no work.
At the distribution center, soldiers and volunteers work side by side, eighteen hours a day.
And in the line, you hear people whispering about the same thing. Not when this will end... but whether it will.
