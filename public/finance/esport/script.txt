Everyone called him talentless. Even his own parents banned him from playing. But nobody knew that every night, he was secretly training in a computer club.
His name was Leo. Fourteen, quiet, always the last one picked. At school, one kid made sure everyone laughed at him. Max. "Leo? He can't even win a game of tag."
At home, the rule was simple. No games. So at midnight, Leo climbed out of his window and ran to a tiny club two streets away. The old owner never asked him for money. He just left the light on.
Leo trained for years. Aim drills. Replays. Thousands of hours. And he kept losing. Local cup, out in the first round. Online qualifier, one point short. He cried on the bus home, then went back the next night.
Then, one message. A team needed a last-minute player for the national finals.
He walked in, and froze. Across the stage sat the captain of the other team. Max.
Final round. Tied. Leo was the last one alive. One against three. The crowd went silent. One shot. Two. Three.
They won.
In the crowd, his father stood up, crying. Because the old club owner was his best friend. Every night, every hour Leo trained there, his father had secretly paid for it.
Would you have forgiven him?
