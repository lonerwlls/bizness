And here's the surprise. Food has never been easier. Plant a seed, add some water, and wheat grows in about twenty minutes. Three wheat make a loaf of bread. Cows and chickens wander the fields, and one steak fills you right up. Bees buzz between square flowers, and fishing takes just a stick and some string. Hunger works differently too. You can feel it, like a bar slowly draining. Run, jump or fight, and it drains faster. But keep it full, and something amazing happens. Your wounds heal on their own. A broken arm is fixed by lunch. Hospitals become quiet. Doctors become farmers. Pharmacies sell just one medicine now, a bucket of milk, which cures almost anything. Supermarkets turn into giant farms, with fields stacked ten floors high. World hunger, one of humanity's oldest problems, disappears in a single season. Restaurants serve only a few dishes, because there are only a few recipes. Bread, steak, cake, mushroom stew. Nobody complains. But all of this raises one terrifying question. What happens when you die?
