On the first night, nobody is ready. Wherever it's dark enough, shapes begin to appear. Tall shadows with glowing eyes. Groaning figures that walk with their arms out. Bony archers that shoot from the treetops. They don't come from anywhere. They simply spawn, in any dark corner. Under bridges, in basements, in unlit parks. People run inside and slam their doors. But doors, it turns out, can be broken. The groaning figures pound on wood until it cracks. Streets that still have torches glow like islands, safe and bright. Everywhere else, people hide and listen. Then they learn the trick. Light. Monsters can't spawn where it's bright. By the second night, every street is covered in torches. Billions of tiny flames, from New York to Tokyo. From space, the Earth has never looked brighter. Astronauts say the planet looks like a glowing chessboard. And at sunrise, the monsters left outside start burning in the sunlight. But surviving the night was the easy part. Now people have to eat.
