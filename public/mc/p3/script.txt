Paper bills turned into flat squares that nobody can eat or build with. Bank accounts vanished with the power. So what is valuable now? Wood. Every tool starts with wood, and the only way to get it is to punch a tree. Yes, punch it. A few seconds of hitting a trunk with your bare hands, and a log pops out, small enough to fit in your pocket. Stone comes next. Then coal, then iron. And deep underground, past the lava, there's diamond. Finding one takes hours of digging in the dark, with lava hissing just one block away. Within a week, diamonds are worth more than houses. Office workers become miners. Bankers dig tunnels under their own skyscrapers. The richest person on Earth is no longer a tech boss, but a teenager who already knew where to dig. Shops reopen, but now you pay in iron. Gold is pretty, but too soft for good tools, so its price crashes. A loaf of bread costs one ingot. A diamond pickaxe could buy a whole street. Everyone is digging. But what you can build with all that stuff is even crazier.
