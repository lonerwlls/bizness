Chop the bottom off a tree, and the rest just hangs there in the air. A bridge loses its middle, and both halves stay floating. Only sand and gravel fall. So the first person who digs under a beach gets buried in seconds. Water is even stranger. Pour one bucket on a hill, and it flows forever, never running out. Within an hour, people figure out that two buckets of water make an endless lake. Builders discover they can stack a tower one block wide and a hundred blocks high, and it never wobbles. Kids build staircases straight into the clouds. Fire trucks need just one bucket. But one bucket can also flood a whole street. Then someone notices the light. A torch made from a stick and a lump of coal glows forever, without any fuel. No electricity needed. Lava, meanwhile, oozes out of cracks in the ground, slow and orange, and it burns anything it touches. By lunchtime, the new rules are clear. Blocks don't fall, water never ends, and light is free. And there's one more problem. Your money just became useless.
