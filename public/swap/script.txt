Seven a.m. You open your eyes, and your feet hang off the end of the bed.
Yesterday, you were short.
Every morning, you wake up in a brand new body. And it's going to be so much weirder than you think.
Rule one: your brain needs a minute. In experiments, people start to feel that a rubber hand is their own in about eleven seconds. A whole new body takes longer. You walk into the doorframe. Twice.
Rule two: breakfast is a gamble. About one in four people is a supertaster. Today, you are one. Broccoli tastes like punishment.
Rule three: your own dog barks at you. Dogs have up to three hundred million smell receptors. We have about six million. To him, you smell like a stranger.
And the last rule, the one nobody warns you about: after a while, you forget which face was yours.
So you keep one thing. Your hat.
Then your best friend spots a stranger in that hat, smiles, and says: "Morning. Who are you today?"
Seven a.m. Your feet hang off the bed. And you don't mind.
