So you decide to help. Your first rescue happens on day three. A car loses its brakes on the hill above the harbor. You see it through two buildings with x-ray vision, reach it before the driver can even scream, and stop it with one hand. The crowd goes wild. That night, a fishing boat flips in a storm. You hear the crew's thoughts from fifty kilometers away, fly through the rain, and carry all of them to shore. You're a hero. But here's the math nobody tells you. Somewhere in the world, a fire, a crash, or a flood is happening every single second. Even flying at the speed of sound, reaching the other side of the planet takes you about sixteen hours. You can't be everywhere. You save one person, and you hear a hundred others you didn't reach. Your super hearing never switches off, so you hear them at night too. You stop sleeping. You stop eating. Your friends stop calling. You can lift a truck, but you can't lift the feeling that it's never enough.
