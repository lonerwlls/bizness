You fly out past the lighthouse, over open water, where nothing can get hurt. And flying is nothing like the movies. At just one hundred kilometers per hour, the wind feels like a wall. Your eyes water, your cheeks flap, and bugs hit your face like tiny rocks. Go higher, and it gets cold fast. Every thousand meters up, the air drops about six degrees. At the height of a passenger jet, it's around minus fifty, and there's barely enough oxygen to stay awake. A normal person would pass out. You don't. Because one of your powers is invulnerability, and it's quietly saving your life every second. Now try super speed. Go faster than sound, and you create a sonic boom, a crack so loud it can shatter windows. So you make rules. Stay over the sea. Slow down near people. Never hit anything you love. By sunset you can hover, land softly, and pick up an egg without breaking it. You fly home feeling unstoppable. Then you land in your street, and you see it. Someone filmed everything.
