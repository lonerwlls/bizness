The first problem is simple. You can't touch anything. You grab the door handle, and it comes off in your hand. You lean on the sink, and it cracks in half. Every move has to be gentle, like you're holding a soap bubble. Your strength isn't a switch. It's always on. Then breakfast. You pour cereal, and with super speed it takes a fraction of a second. Milk sprays across the kitchen like a fire hose. To anyone watching, you basically teleported. Your phone buzzes. You tap the screen, and your finger goes straight through it. That's phone number one. By lunch, you've broken four. Outside, it's even worse. Super hearing means every car horn feels like a scream. Every seagull sounds like an alarm. Your x-ray eyes flicker on and off, so the whole street keeps turning into skeletons and pipes. Here's the scary part. Heroes in movies get years to learn. You got one morning. And one sneeze right now could knock a house off its foundation. So you do the only smart thing. You go somewhere empty, and you practice.
