Three days without food deliveries. Tonight, you found food. And the whole building knows.
The news warned you. Don't hoard. Don't tell anyone what you have.
The supermarket downstairs has been empty since Monday. Bare shelves. A broken door.
So at midnight, you go down with a flashlight. And in the back room, behind the boxes, you find it. One crate. Rice. Beans. Soup. Enough for a month.
You carry it up the stairs. On the third floor, a door opens a crack. Someone sees the crate. The door closes. Quietly.
Fifth floor. Apartment twelve. You lock the door. And you cook. Just one pot.
That's your mistake. In a building where nobody has eaten in two days, smell travels.
Your phone buzzes. The building chat. Who's cooking on five? Twelve people typing.
You turn off the stove. Too late.
Footsteps on the stairs. Not one person. Many.
A knock. We know you have food. Open the door.
Part two tomorrow. Follow, so you don't miss it.
