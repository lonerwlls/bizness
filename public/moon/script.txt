Midnight. You step outside, and the Moon looks... bigger. Not a little. A lot.
Tonight, the Moon has started falling toward Earth. And it's going to be so much weirder than you think.
Rule one: the tides go wild. Right now, the Moon drifts about four centimeters farther away every year. Pull it in to half the distance, and its pull on the oceans gets eight times stronger. Your beach is now a lake. Twice a day.
Rule two: nights stop being dark. At half the distance, a full Moon looks twice as wide and shines four times as bright. You can read a book at midnight. You can't sleep at all.
Rule three: your days get shorter. Four hundred million years ago, when the Moon was closer, a day lasted only about twenty-two hours. Your weekend just lost four hours.
And the last rule: there's a line it can't cross. About eighteen thousand kilometers out, Earth's gravity tears the Moon apart. It becomes rings, like Saturn's.
So you step outside one more time.
Midnight. The Moon is gone. And the whole sky is glowing.
