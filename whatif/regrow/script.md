# What If You Could Regrow Any Body Part?
Medical "What if" #2 · hero: Doctor ROO · narrator "you" · English · target ~9 min

## 1. Cold open — the bagel
001 It's three in the morning, and you're slicing a bagel in the hospital break room. The knife slips. — *night break room, 3D bagel and knife, the slip*
002 Half an inch of your finger is just… gone. — *hero stares at his hand (cartoon: bandage-white stump, no blood), nurse freezes*
003 You're a doctor. You know exactly what to do. Ice, gauze, call the surgeon. But before you can move, your finger starts to itch. — *he grabs gauze, stops, looks at the finger*
004 Then it grows. Bone, then skin, then a tiny new nail. Forty seconds. Not even a scar. — *close-up: fingertip grows back in 3 stages, sparkle*
005 The night nurse drops her coffee. You drop the bagel. — *two drops, coffee splash on the floor, bagel rolls and stays*
006 So what if you could regrow any part of your body? — *title words, hero in a spotlight looking at his hand*
007 Sounds impossible? Not for everyone. An axolotl, a smiling little salamander from Mexico, can regrow a leg, part of its heart, even pieces of its brain. Without a single scar. — *aquarium: 3D axolotl, its leg grows back; labels LEG / HEART / BRAIN*
008 In eighteen ninety-eight, scientist Thomas Hunt Morgan cut a flatworm into tiny pieces. A piece just one two-hundred-seventy-ninth of the worm grew into a whole new worm. — *sepia lab 1898, Morgan at a microscope, worm sliced, "1/279" pops, piece becomes a worm*
009 Today, that's you. Only you're a doctor. — *Doctor ROO in a white coat, "THAT'S YOU" / "DOCTOR" tags, counter appears: REGROWN 1*

## 2. Three rules (experiments on yourself)
010 First, you need to know how it works. So you do what any scientist would do. You experiment on yourself. — *small lab, ROO rolls up a sleeve, notebook*
011 Your colleague, Dr. Reyes, takes a tiny slice of skin from your arm, then stitches the wound shut, nice and neat. — *Reyes (not yellow, not ginger) with tweezers, needle and thread*
012 Nothing happens. A week later, you just have a scar. — *calendar page flips 7 days, scar line on the arm*
013 So she takes another slice, and this time leaves the wound open under a bandage. Twenty seconds later, the skin is back. — *bandage, timer 0:20, bandage off: clean skin*
014 Rule number one: a stitched wound scars. An open one regrows. — *rule card: needle = scar, bandage = regrow*
015 Weirdly, real doctors noticed something similar. In nineteen seventy-four, Dr. Cynthia Illingworth in Sheffield reported that children's cut-off fingertips often grew back, if surgeons just left them alone instead of stitching them closed. — *1974 card, children's hospital, child's finger in a dressing*
016 It works in kids up to about eleven years old. Nail, fingerprint and all. — *age bar 0–11, fingertip with a fingerprint*
017 Rule number two shows up at lunch. You're starving. You eat two burgers, a pizza, and half the vending machine. — *cafeteria: he eats with his hands, stack of empty plates*
018 A new body part has to be built from something, and that something is whatever you eat. Every regrowth costs you a mountain of food. — *infographic: plate → arrow → finger*
019 Rule number three shows up at a softball game. A ball knocks out your front tooth. By dinner, a new one has pushed in. — *park, ball hits, tooth flies and lands on the grass, dinner table, new tooth*
020 Not a copy of the old tooth. A brand-new one. No filling, no stains. Whatever regrows comes back factory new. — *old tooth (filling, stain) vs new tooth, "FACTORY NEW" stamp inside the card*
021 Real scientists are chasing this exact trick. In twenty twenty-four, doctors in Kyoto began the first human trial of a drug meant to help people grow new teeth. They hope it could reach patients around twenty thirty. — *Kyoto clinic card, 2024 → 2030 timeline*

## 3. The ladder — the Spare Parts Doctor
022 Now, a doctor who can regrow anything starts asking a dangerous question. What else could I give away? — *ROO at an X-ray light box, looking at his own body*
023 Here's a real fact: the human liver regrows. A living donor can give part of theirs to a patient, and in about two months it grows back to almost full size. — *liver diagram: part removed, grows back, "~2 MONTHS"*
024 Yours grows back overnight. — *same diagram, timer "1 NIGHT"*
025 So you become a donor. A slice of liver in March. Another in April. By December, you've given away more than one whole liver, and you're still using yours. — *wall calendar months flip, liver icons pile up*
026 Then you try a kidney. A normal kidney can't regrow at all. Yours is back in four days. — *kidney card, days 1–4 ticking*
027 The waiting list at your hospital gets shorter every week. The newspapers call you "the Spare Parts Doctor." — *waiting-list board losing names; newspaper held in hands*
028 And companies come calling. A man in a very expensive suit offers you a billion dollars for a vial of your cells. — *office, 3D vial, check with many zeros*
029 Real labs are already trying to grow body parts. In two thousand six, Dr. Anthony Atala reported seven young patients living with bladders grown from their own cells. — *lab: cells → scaffold → bladder, "7 PATIENTS, 2006"*
030 And in twenty twenty-two, a young woman in Texas received an ear that was 3D-printed from her own cartilage cells. — *3D printer printing an ear, 2022 tag*
031 But real labs are slow, and you're not. You turn down the man in the suit. You've got patients waiting. — *he slides the check back, walks out to the ward*

## 4. The price
032 Then, one Tuesday, Dr. Reyes looks at your blood test and goes quiet. — *Reyes at a screen, her face drops*
033 Your cells are dividing faster than anything she's ever seen. Faster than a deer antler. — *microscope view of fast-dividing cells*
034 Antlers are one of the fastest-growing tissues of any mammal. Up to two centimeters a day. — *forest, deer, antler growing with a ruler "2 CM/DAY"*
035 And in twenty nineteen, scientists found that antlers grow using genes that look a lot like the genes of bone cancer. The deer just know how to keep them in check. — *gene strip: ANTLER vs BONE CANCER, matching bars*
036 Fast growth and cancer are two sides of the same coin. In nineteen eighty-six, a doctor named Harold Dvorak described tumors as "wounds that do not heal." — *quote card, sepia note*
037 "Every time you regrow something," Reyes says, "you roll the dice." — *3D dice roll on the desk and stay there*
038 You start counting. Forty-one regrowths. Forty-one rolls of the dice. — *counter above his head ticks to 41*
039 Three weeks later, you find it. A small, hard lump on your forearm, right where that second slice of skin grew back. — *bathroom mirror, morning, he feels his arm*
040 Your hands shake while Reyes cuts it out. It isn't cancer. Not yet. It's just skin that didn't know when to stop. — *procedure room, lab report "BENIGN"*
041 This time, she stitches the wound shut. On purpose. A scar is a stop sign. — *needle and thread, "STOP" sign-shaped scar card*
042 Maybe that's why we scar at all. One theory says mammals traded regrowing for something faster and safer: a quick patch that stops infection, and keeps runaway growth in check. — *axolotl vs human card, "THEORY" label*

## 5. The choice
043 So you decide to stop. No more regrowing. Ever. — *he puts the counter away in a drawer*
044 And that's exactly when Lily arrives. She's seven. Her liver is failing, and there's no donor for her on the list. — *ER doors, a little girl on a raised bed, half-sitting, eyes open*
045 Except you. Same blood type. A perfect match. — *match card: two blood bags, check mark*
046 A normal donor would grow that piece back over a couple of months. You'll grow it back in one night. Another roll of the dice. — *dice on the table again*
047 Reyes sits down next to you in the empty cafeteria. "You don't owe anyone this," she says. — *night cafeteria, two people sitting properly on chairs*
048 You look at the bagel on your tray. The same kind that started all of this. — *3D bagel on the tray, close-up*
049 Then you sign the form. — *pen in hand, signature*
050 The surgery takes nine hours. By morning, Lily's new liver is working. And yours has already grown back. — *OR, clock 9 hours, monitor beats; dawn*
051 For a week, you check your skin every morning. No lumps. The counter stops at forty-two. And that's where you leave it. — *mirror, counter 42, he turns off the light*

## 6. The real twist
052 Here's the twist, though. You don't need a superpower to regrow body parts. You already can. A little. — *ROO faces camera, the counter fades*
053 Your liver regrows. Your skin keeps replacing itself. A broken bone rebuilds itself, and a rib can even grow back, if surgeons leave its outer lining in place. — *x-ray infographic lighting up liver, skin, bone, rib*
054 Cut nerves regrow too, about a millimeter a day. And kids' fingertips grow back, if we just leave them alone. — *nerve line growing, "1 MM/DAY"*
055 The power isn't missing. It's switched off. And scientists are looking for the switch. — *big switch in the OFF position*
056 In twenty twenty-two, a team led by Michael Levin at Tufts University put a tiny dome filled with five drugs over a frog's cut leg, for just twenty-four hours. Over the next eighteen months, the frog regrew a working leg. — *lab: frog, dome, 24 h, months flip, leg regrows*
057 It wasn't perfect. But a frog that shouldn't regrow a leg did. And mammals are next on the list. — *frog swims with the new leg*

## 7. Finale
058 A year later, Lily is back in your ER. She caught her finger in a car door. The tip is gone. — *ER, Lily (same look), bandaged finger*
059 The surgeon on duty reaches for the needle and thread. You stop him. "Leave it open. Just a dressing." — *hand stops the needle*
060 Eight weeks later, Lily shows you a brand-new fingertip. Nail and all. No superpower needed. — *Lily waves the finger, ROO smiles*
061 So if you could regrow one body part, which one would you pick? Tell me in the comments. — *question card with ROO*
062 And subscribe. — *subscribe button (3D)*
