There isn't a single clock in a casino. And that's no accident.

Imagine you walk in "just for 10 minutes." $20 in your pocket, and the last bus leaves at 9.

Inside, it's always evening: no windows, soft lights, music that never stops. A waiter hands you a free juice. "Thanks!"

You win. You lose. You win again. You go to check the time, but your phone died hours ago.

You step outside, and the sun is coming up. How is that possible?

Think about the last time you opened a video "for 5 minutes" before bed, and suddenly it was 3 a.m. Nothing around you reminded you of the time.

Casinos do this on purpose. Without clocks or windows, your brain loses track of time, and the longer you play, the more money you leave behind.

Back in 2000, former casino manager Bill Friedman wrote these rules down in a book. Plenty of casinos are still built this way.

Oh, and that 9 o’clock bus? It left. Yesterday.
